The house

A mill, a grate, a city that eats late.

Ember & Olive is not a concept. It is Lina Haddad’s answer to cooking in Barcelona without pretending the eastern Mediterranean needs a tasting-menu costume. The oil still arrives in tins. The fire is older than the room.

Koura, then Paris

She learned heat before she learned kitchens.

Lina grew up next to the family mill in Koura, north of Beirut, where November smells of crushed fruit and wet stone. Stages in Paris taught her how to butcher and how to keep her mouth shut. Barcelona taught her that dinner can start at ten and still be serious. She opened this door in 2019 with twenty covers and a second-hand grill that still works.

The brigade

Eight people. No pass shouting.

Marc Vila keeps the list short on purpose: Greek island whites, Lebanese bottles that don’t need a speech, Catalan growers who still farm their own fruit. Pastry is a misnomer — Nour handles the cold sweets and the bread. The rest of the crew rotates between fire, prep, and floor because a 42-cover room cannot afford specialists who won’t carry plates.

Notes in the ledger

2014

Lina leaves the mill winters for a stage in the 11th. Writes home that Paris butter is a conspiracy.

2017

A pop-up in a Poble-sec garage. The octopus dish appears and refuses to leave.

2019

Carrer del Rec opens. Twenty covers, one grill, a Monday off that nobody believed.

2024

The cellar is finished. The terrace gets a canvas. The mill still sends the November oil.

How we buy

Markets before suppliers.

Fish is chosen at the stall, not from a list. Lamb comes from a single farm in Ripollès. Citrus in winter is from València because it tastes like itself. We will not fly in a garnish. If the Palamós prawns are ugly that morning, the card says so.
